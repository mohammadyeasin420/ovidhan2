1. What Are Break Collocations?

Collocations are combinations of words that naturally go together. For example, English commonly uses "break a record". We can also say "smash a record" when someone beats a record by a large margin. Learning collocations helps your English sound more natural and idiomatic.

Break collocations include natural combinations where the verb "break" is paired with nouns, fixed expressions, and common phrasal verbs. Examples include "break a record", "break the ice", and "break down".

6. Break vs Crack vs Smash – Understanding the Differences

These verbs are often confused. Here is how to use them correctly with Bangla explanations.

VerbBangla MeaningNatural Example
Breakসাধারণভাবে ভেঙে যাওয়া / ভাঙা"I broke the window."
Crackফাটল তৈরি করা"The stone cracked the window."
Smashজোরে সম্পূর্ণভাবে ভেঙে ফেলা"He smashed the glass."
Splitদুই/একাধিক অংশে ভাগ হওয়া"The wood split in two."

💡 Tip: Use break for general damage, crack for a line of damage, smash for violent destruction, and split for dividing into parts.

7. 🔄 Collocation Pairs: Keep ↔ Break

Learn these contrasting pairs to understand how "keep" and "break" create opposite meanings.

Keep a promise ↔ Break a promise প্রতিশ্রুতি রাখা ↔ প্রতিশ্রুতি ভাঙা "I kept my promise." ↔ "He broke his promise."
Keep quiet ↔ Break the silence চুপ থাকা ↔ নীরবতা ভাঙা "Please keep quiet." ↔ "He broke the silence with a laugh."

8. Common Mistakes with Break Collocations

❌ Wrong: "The machine broken down."
✅ Correct: "The machine broke down."
💡 Explanation: Use the past tense "broke down", not "broken down".
❌ Wrong: "He break the rules."
✅ Correct: "He broke the rules."
💡 Explanation: Use the past tense form "broke" or present "breaks".

10. Frequently Asked Questions

What are break collocations? ▾
Break collocations are natural combinations where the verb "break" is paired with nouns, fixed expressions, and common phrasal verbs. Examples include "break a record", "break the ice", and "break down".
What is the difference between "break" and "crack"? ▾
"Crack" means to form a line of breakage without fully shattering. "Break" means to separate into pieces. For example, a cracked window is still intact; a broken window is in pieces.
Why are break collocations important for IELTS? ▾
Using a wide range of collocations like "break new ground" helps you demonstrate a broader and more natural lexical range in speaking and writing.
Is this useful for BCS and Bank exams? ▾
Yes. Collocations are useful for vocabulary, sentence correction, and natural English usage in competitive-exam preparation.
