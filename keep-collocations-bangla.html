1. What Are Keep Collocations?

Collocations are combinations of words that naturally go together. For example, in English we say "keep calm", not "maintain calm". Learning collocations helps your English sound more natural and idiomatic.

Keep collocations are phrases where the verb "keep" is paired with a noun, adjective, or preposition. They are incredibly common in everyday English, IELTS writing, and competitive exams like BCS and Bank Jobs.

Note: English commonly uses both "keep calm" and "stay calm". "Keep calm" often emphasises continuing to remain calm, while "stay calm" emphasises maintaining a state of calm. Both are correct and natural.

6. Keep vs Other Verbs (Bangla Focus)

Bangla speakers often confuse "keep" with "put", "stay", or "hold". Here is how to use them correctly.

Bangla MeaningWrong (❌)Correct (✅)Explanation
জিনিসপত্র রাখা"I keep my books on the table." (if you mean "place")"I put my books on the table."Use "put" for placing. Use "keep" for storing or holding permanently.
চুপ থাকা"Stay quiet." (not wrong, but different emphasis)"Keep quiet.""Keep quiet" is more common in British English. "Stay quiet" is also correct.
নিরাপদে রাখা"I hold the money safe.""I keep the money safe.""Hold" means to grip. "Keep" means to store or protect.

7. Common Mistakes with Keep Collocations (Bangladesh Focus)

❌ Wrong: "I am keeping the food on the table."
✅ Correct: "I am putting the food on the table."
💡 Explanation: Use "keep" for long-term storage. For temporary placement, use "put".
❌ Wrong: "I want to keep contact with my friends."
✅ Correct: "I want to keep in touch with my friends."
💡 Explanation: The correct collocation is "keep in touch", not "keep contact".

9. Frequently Asked Questions

What are collocations? ▾
Collocations are combinations of words that naturally go together. For example, we say "keep calm" not "maintain calm". Learning collocations helps you sound more natural and fluent in English.
What are keep collocations? ▾
Keep collocations are phrases where the verb "keep" is paired with a noun, adjective, or preposition. Examples include "keep in touch", "keep calm", "keep an eye on", and "keep going".
Why are keep collocations important for IELTS? ▾
Using a wide range of collocations like "keep in mind" or "keep track of" helps you demonstrate a broader and more natural lexical range in speaking and writing.
What is the difference between "keep" and "put"? ▾
"Keep" means to hold, retain, or continue. "Put" means to place or position something. For example, "I keep my keys in my bag" (hold/store) vs "I put my keys on the table" (place).
Is this useful for BCS and Bank exams? ▾
Yes. Collocations are useful for vocabulary, sentence correction, and natural English usage in competitive-exam preparation.
